The Long Run

        How a match works

        Nobody is driving. Each fighter is an orb bouncing inside the arena holding a weapon against its shell — the weapon is a free point mass on a hard distance constraint, driven tangentially, so it whips and lags instead of being pinned to a rotating transform. A weapon is a segment, and when it overlaps an enemy orb it lands a hit, capped to one hit per target per swing. When two enemy weapons overlap each other instead, that is a parry: neither lands, both rebound, and each side briefly loses tempo. Reach is not free — weapons sit against the orb unless a template (the Lancer), a perk (Extended Grip) or a powerup (Long Chain) pushes them out onto a chain.

        Damage runs through a matchup multiplier, the attacker's passive, and the defender's armour, then the attacking template's onHit applies its signature effect. Ultimate meters fill from hits and from time, and fire the instant they cap. The whole simulation is a fixed 120 Hz and draws every random number from a seeded generator, which is why a seed replays exactly.
