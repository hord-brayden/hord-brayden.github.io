The spirit of the law

Laws were written about outcomes.

Consent laws were passed because people's data was being shared against their wishes. Checking banner configuration, consent categories and tag rules is necessary. It doesn't show whether the data actually stayed put. Faraday is built to show that, and it does it without sending a single test request to a vendor.

On the roadmap

The question nobody can answer from outside

Does an opt-out on one site actually stop the data from reaching that site's partners?

Roadmap · needs legal review

Foolia opts out on a host site, then visits that site's ad partners in the same browser profile. The test checks whether a partner's ad stack acts like it already knows her, or receives an ID only the host could have created. That would be direct evidence of the cross-business sharing that consent laws restrict.

Public seller and inventory files help pick which partners to test. They show who can sell a site's ad space. They don't show data sharing.

Why it isn't built yet

Partner sites aren't our customers. Before this ships, a lawyer reviews each in-scope site's terms of service, and the tool is limited to acting as an ordinary visitor: no bypassing bot checks and no non-public systems. Findings will be framed for the customer as "your vendors are leaking," never as an accusation against a third party.

What's hard to copy

The engine, not the features

Most features on a datasheet can be cloned. These parts take real time to get right.

A

A boundary that holds

The browser starts with every hostname resolving to nothing. Every byte passes through one interceptor. Stray preconnects, DNS-encoded data and peer-to-peer STUN requests fail closed.

B

Replay that matches

Randomness is seeded and the clock is frozen inside the page. Record once, replay twice, and diff the requests. A finding you can rerun is one a customer can trust.

C

Canaries that can't hide

Fabricated persona values are searched for in every payload: raw, URL-encoded, base64 at any offset, hex, and three hash formats.
